Learning evidence and claims

What app records can show and what they cannot establish.

Hamartia helps organize practice and retrieval. Its interface reports submitted records; it does not measure all learning or establish that a particular routine will improve grades.

What the records show

Exam results show values entered for a selected exam. Practice trends summarize completed sets. Review history reflects the student's own correct/incorrect marks. Pattern counts cover saved mistakes and available labels/order metadata. A Clock duration shows recorded elapsed time, not attention or learning quality.

Recording a learning reflection

In Study Plan, choose one of the suggested topics and enter the activity you completed, your observation, the date, and an optional reference link. The reference must use HTTP(S) and pass hostname syntax checks. It opens only if you click it, and its visible label shows the host as your browser parses it. Hamartia does not fetch links or check their availability or content; check the displayed host and open only references you trust. This separate record is your own report; it does not change review scheduling, exam net, or XP. Hamartia does not verify that the activity happened, grade the reflection, or infer learning or mastery from it.

What they do not prove

These records do not provide a complete denominator for unsaved questions, independently grade answers, verify a curriculum match, or demonstrate mastery from a single result. AI-generated advice is a suggestion that may be wrong; it does not mark tasks complete or certify outcomes. Time in the app, streaks, XP, and timer use are not evidence of improved learning.

Study design context

Retrieval and distributed practice have evidence of average benefits in classroom research, with variation by task and outcome. Concrete plans can support follow-through in studied contexts, but results do not guarantee effects in Hamartia. Fixed Pomodoro intervals have not been established as universally superior. Read the cited studies and their limits before generalizing:

  • Retrieval, distributed, and interleaved practice in authentic classrooms (2026 review).
  • Planning and studying micro-randomized trial (2025).
  • Pomodoro, Flowtime, and self-regulated breaks (2025 study).

Interpreting progress

Use trends to choose a next practice step, then check understanding with fresh questions and explanations. Treat summaries as prompts for reflection, not judgments about a student. See Mistakes and review.
